# Prose and editing

## Fit the medium

| Medium | Reader outcome and useful treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Article, essay, newsletter | Develop a supported idea with examples, a through-line, and the author's voice |
| Report, proposal, memo | Make the finding or decision clear; put evidence, qualifications, and consequences nearby |
| Email or other message | State the request, decision, or update; identify owner and timing when known; preserve appropriate warmth |
| UI copy | Identify the action or state, use consistent terms, and give actionable recovery without inventing a cause |
| Academic, legal, medical, safety, or policy text | Preserve evidence, defined terms, calibrated claims, required notices, and governing conventions |
| Marketing | Explain substantiated value to a specific reader and make the next action clear |
| Speech or slides | Use spoken rhythm, clear transitions, and useful repetition; keep visual density suitable for listening |
| Narrative or personal writing | Preserve perspective, deliberate ambiguity, scene, and genuine experience |

Treat these as reader needs, not mandatory templates. Follow the author's
supplied samples for voice and the destination's conventions for form.
Spelling follows the user or house style; do not silently Americanise a draft.

## Edit in consequence order

1. **Meaning.** Inventory claims, sources, quantities, uncertainty, obligations,
   conditions, warnings, and links. Preserve their force.
2. **Substance.** Identify the useful point and the evidence or experience
   behind it. Ask about a consequential gap or mark it for the author.
3. **Development.** Connect paragraphs through an actual relationship. Give
   prerequisites and examples where the reader needs them.
4. **Sentences.** Replace empty abstraction with the known actor, action,
   mechanism, or consequence. Keep ordinary words and necessary terminology.
5. **Voice.** Preserve warmth, rhythm, and distinctive language that serves the
   piece. Stop editing when further changes merely homogenise it.

For a review, locate the consequential issues and suggest the smallest repair.
For a rewrite, respect the requested extent of change. For a new piece, use
the available brief and source material without requiring a ritual interview.

## Meaning checks

- Keep “may reduce latency” uncertain. Keep “should retry” a recommendation.
  Keep correlation distinct from causation and a vendor claim attributed.
- Preserve technical distinctions: configuration, an option, and a setting are
  not automatically interchangeable. Prefer a clear explanation to a lossy swap.
- A passive sentence can be useful when the actor is unknown or unimportant.
  A longer sentence can express a condition more clearly than chopped fragments.
- Make examples concrete from evidence, or explicitly hypothetical. Do not
  invent a customer's quote, a personal anecdote, a study, or a performance figure.
- Keep useful repetition in a warning, speech, or reference entry. Remove
  repetition that asks the reader to process the same point without benefit.

Read for patterns in context: generic importance claims, vague attribution,
unearned superlatives, repeated rhetorical formulas, and filler introductions.
Repair the thought rather than swapping one fashionable synonym for another.
Never manufacture mistakes, slang, or personal history to appear human.
